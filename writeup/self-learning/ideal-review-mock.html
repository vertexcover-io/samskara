Part 1 — What the current review gets wrong

The review that rendered on your screen is accurate — I checked its claims against the transcript and every citation resolves. The problems are what's missing, what it blames, and how it reads. Six findings:

  1. More than half of the review was silently thrown away. The reviewer's own last message (seq 128) says it wrote 20 timeline entries and 5 human + 6 agent learnings. What persisted is 8 entries and 2 + 2 — a 6 KB payload. The XML healing step recovered what it could and dropped the rest without a word. The page shows 8 entries as if that were the whole review, so you judged a mutilated deliverable.

  2. It blames you for our bug. The top "human learning" is "Ship the export you promise" — but the missing session.json was a samskara pipeline failure from that era (the exporter never wrote the file into the workspace; since fixed). The lens set has two audiences, human and agent, so a harness failure has nowhere to go and gets laundered into "your prompt was bad advice".

  3. The review carries no numbers. Not duration, not tokens, not tool-call counts, not what the detour cost. The session burned 95 of 278 seconds (34%) recovering from the missing file before any analysis started — the single most important fact about this session, and it appears nowhere. Without numbers, "moderate friction" is an opinion.

  4. The timeline is prose wearing a timeline costume. Entries have seq ranges but no clock time, no duration, no share of session. Nothing answers "where did the time go". Tags like review task-setup are taxonomy for us, noise for you.

  5. Learnings describe the past, never the next time. "Define the fallback for a missing artifact" is advice-shaped but abstract: no cost, no imperative, no repeat context (is this the first time or the fifth?). And the sharpest learning in the session — the agent validated its grounding against a reconstruction it built itself, which is circular — is buried inside the summary paragraph and never becomes a learning at all. That's your "a learning that isn't isolated as one".

  6. Timeline entries don't navigate. Clicking one does nothing (verified headless). The deep-links you were promised exist only on the small seq chips inside learnings. The verdict stamps (SHIPPED MODERATE) float with no one-line "why" bound to them — the why lives in a dense summary paragraph.

Part 3 — What has to change where The mapping from findings to work, so alignment on Part 2 decides all of it.

  1. Lens schema (core): timeline entries gain wall-clock start + duration; learnings gain cost, severity, next-time line, and a third audience harness; the review gains a numbers block (duration, tokens, tool calls) computed from the session, not generated by the reviewer.

  2. Prompt (core): attribution rules — harness failures are named as harness, never reworded into human advice; numbers come pre-computed so the reviewer cites instead of estimating; self-certification forbidden; "no learning" must be stated explicitly, not implied by silence.

  3. Pipeline (server): the reviewer's self-reported counts (it already emits them) are checked against what parses — a mismatch either fails the job or persists with a visible partial banner like the one above. Silent loss becomes impossible.

  4. Web: verdict line with its one-sentence why; the time-flow strip; skim/collapse timeline rows wired as deep-links; learning cards with the four slots; the partial banner.

Part 4 — Decisions I need from you

  1. Harness as a third audience — separate learnings about samskara itself, so they stop landing on you. Yes?

  2. Partial reviews: when emitted counts ≠ persisted counts, hard-fail the job (rerun needed, no partial state) or persist with the banner? My vote: banner + auto-rerun once, fail if it repeats.

  3. The "YOU: nothing to change" card — always render an explicit empty-verdict for the human, or omit when there's nothing? My vote: always render; silence reads as "didn't check".

  4. Severity vocabulary — the three-level low/medium/high on cost (time, tokens, trust), or no severity at all in v1? My vote: keep it, it drives sorting.

  5. Timeline granularity — 5 phases like above (skimmable) vs the current 8+ entries with detail collapsed. My vote: reviewer emits phases + turning points only; the UI never shows more than ~7 rows unexpanded.
